The paper

Format of the paper. The paper covers the whole module, Sessions 1 to 8, and its questions are of the same three kinds as those on the exercise sheets: some ask you to carry out a computation (row reduce a matrix, find an inverse, compute eigenvalues and eigenvectors, fit a line by least squares); some ask you to decide a question and justify the answer (is this set of vectors linearly independent, does this system have a solution for every right-hand side, is this matrix diagonalisable); and some ask you to set up a small engineering problem as a matrix equation before solving it. The marks available for each question, and for each part of a question, are printed on the paper.

Assessment criteria. Marks are awarded for the following, listed in decreasing order of weight.

  • Method, clearly set out. A row reduction with every step visible, a definition applied correctly, a theorem named when it is used. The method carries most of the marks for a question; a correct final number with no working carries very few.
  • Correct final result. An arithmetic slip is penalised once, where it occurs. If the method is right, the rest of the question is marked on your own numbers, so an early slip does not cost you the whole question.
  • Justification. When a question asks whether something is true (is this matrix invertible, is this set linearly independent), a bare yes or no receives no credit. The mark is for the reason: the pivot count, the determinant, the dependence relation you found.
  • Precision. Definitions and theorems should be stated as on the slides, in the wording of the textbook. A statement that is false loses marks even when the final answer is right; a statement that is true but irrelevant to the question earns nothing.
  • Interpretation. Where a computation answers an engineering question (the currents in a circuit, the long-run size of a population, the stationary distribution of a chain), a sentence saying what the numbers mean is part of the answer.

In short: show your working, say why, and use the words the module uses.
